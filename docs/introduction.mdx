AppBlips builds apps, websites and games from a description. You type what you want, as you'd explain it to a friend, and an AI writes the code. A few moments later your project runs in a preview next to the chat, ready to use.

It's made for anyone with an idea: students, teachers, small-business owners, designers, hobbyists and developers who want a quick prototype. You never have to look at code, but it's always there if you want to read it, change it or take it elsewhere.

## What you can build

When you start AppBlips, you choose what to make:

- **Mobile & Web App:** tools and utilities that work on a phone or a computer, such as a budget tracker, a quiz, a timer or a to-do list.
- **Responsive Website:** websites with one or more pages and a menu, such as a portfolio, a restaurant site or a landing page for an event. They adjust to any screen size.
- **Browser Game:** 2D and 3D games you play in the browser, with keyboard and touch controls.

Below the studios are three more options:

- **My projects:** open something you made before.
- **Import a site:** keep working on a site you already have, such as an HTML page, a website `.zip` or a React + Vite project. See [Import a site](/features/importing-sites).
- **Explore:** play with projects other people built with AppBlips, and share your own. See [Showcase](/features/showcase).

## How a build works

<Steps>
  <Step title="You describe it">
    Type your idea in the message box. You can also speak it with the microphone, or attach a screenshot or sketch for the AI to work from.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The AI writes it">
    AppBlips sends your request to your AI provider. While the AI writes, you can watch the code appear.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You try it">
    When the build finishes, your project runs in a live preview. The preview is sandboxed, which means it's kept separate from the rest of your computer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You refine it">
    Ask for changes in plain English, as many times as you like. Each change is saved as a new version that you can undo or go back to.
  </Step>
</Steps>

When you're done, [download your project](/features/exporting) or [publish it to the web](/features/publishing). The code is yours to keep and use however you like.

## What you need

AppBlips uses an AI to write code, so it needs a way to reach one. You have two options:

- **Your own API key.** An API key is a password-like code from an AI company (an "AI provider"), such as Anthropic or OpenAI. You create one on the provider's website, paste it into AppBlips, and the provider bills you for what you use. See [Choose an AI provider](/ai-providers).
- **Free.** Some copies of AppBlips, including the one at [web.appblips.com](https://web.appblips.com), come with AI already set up. Pick **Free (no key needed)** and start building. Free AI is shared by everyone using that copy, so it has limits.

AppBlips itself is free. There's no account to create.

## Ways to run AppBlips

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="In your browser" icon="globe" href="/quickstart-browser">
    The quickest way to try it. Open web.appblips.com and start building. Your projects stay in your browser.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Desktop app" icon="download" href="/quickstart-desktop">
    The best everyday option on Windows and Linux. Projects are saved as folders, and your key is protected by your computer's keychain.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Mac (with Pinokio)" icon="apple" href="/quickstart-desktop#on-a-mac">
    Install the browser version on a Mac in a few clicks with the free Pinokio app.
  </Card>
  <Card title="From source or Docker" icon="code" href="/quickstart-source">
    Run your own copy on any computer with Node.js or [Docker](/self-hosting/docker).
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Wherever you run it, your projects stay with you: on your computer, or in your own browser. AppBlips doesn't keep a copy of them anywhere else.
