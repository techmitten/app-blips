Quick answers to the questions people ask most about AppBlips.

## About AppBlips

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What can AppBlips build?">
    Apps, websites and 2D or 3D browser games, from a description in plain English. For example: tools, calculators, quizzes, habit trackers, dashboards, portfolio sites, business websites and arcade games.

    Apps and games are single HTML files that run in any web browser. Websites can have several pages and export as a `.zip`. Apps from the App studio can also export as a React project.

    AppBlips doesn't build native phone apps or apps that need a server of their own. To save what visitors send, such as RSVPs or votes, try the [Google Sheets Backend](/features/connectors#google-sheets-backend) extension.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do I need to know how to code?">
    No. If you can describe what you want, you can build it. If you do code, you can read, change and export everything AppBlips writes.

    Clear, specific prompts get better first results. Say what should happen when someone clicks a button, what the page should show, and how it should look.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is AppBlips free?">
    Yes. AppBlips is free and open source under the [MIT License](https://github.com/techmitten/app-blips/blob/master/LICENSE). You can use, change and share it, including for commercial work, as long as you keep the copyright and license notice.

    The AI is the only part that can cost money. With your own API key, your AI provider bills you for what you use. On [web.appblips.com](/quickstart-browser), the **Free** option lets you build without a key, within daily limits.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is there a limit on how many apps I can build?">
    AppBlips sets no limit of its own. With your own key, only your AI provider's limits and your budget apply. The **Free** option has limits per hour and per day, set by whoever runs it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What's the difference between the desktop app and the browser version?">
    They're the same app, kept in different places:

    - **[Desktop app](/quickstart-desktop)** (Windows and Linux): saves your projects as folders on your computer, keeps your key in your system's keychain, and updates itself.
    - **Browser version** ([web.appblips.com](/quickstart-browser), [from source](/quickstart-source) or [Docker](/self-hosting/docker)): saves your projects and keys in your web browser, for that address only.

    To move projects between them, use **Settings → Data → Export** in one and **Import…** in the other.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I use AppBlips on a Mac?">
    Yes, through the browser version. The simplest way is [web.appblips.com](/quickstart-browser), with nothing to install. To run your own copy, install it with one click through [Pinokio](https://pinokio.co/apps/github-com-techmitten-app-blips-pinokio), then click **Install**, **Start** and **Open AppBlips** in Pinokio. You can also [run it from source](/quickstart-source) or with [Docker](/self-hosting/docker). There's no Mac desktop app.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## AI and API keys

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I need an API key?">
    Usually, yes. An API key is a secret code from an AI provider that lets AppBlips use your account there. You get one from the provider's website and paste it into AppBlips once. See [Choose an AI provider](/ai-providers).

    You don't need one when the copy of AppBlips you use offers **Free (no key needed)**, as [web.appblips.com](/quickstart-browser) does. Whoever runs that copy has set up AI for everyone using it, with limits.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Which AI providers can I use?">
    Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini, xAI, DeepSeek and OpenRouter. Open **Settings → AI**, choose your provider, paste your API key, choose a model and click **Save**. See [Choose an AI provider](/ai-providers).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What happens to my API key?">
    - **Desktop app:** encrypted with your system's keychain and saved only on your computer. Only the app's background process uses it.
    - **Browser version:** saved in your web browser only. Each request sends it to the AppBlips server, which passes it to your provider and never stores it.

    In every version, your key is never written into the apps you build, your projects or anything you export. See [Security](/self-hosting/security#your-api-key).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How can I see how much I'm using?">
    AppBlips doesn't show a count. Your AI provider's usage or billing page shows what you used and what it cost. See [How much does a build use?](/features/building-apps#how-much-does-a-build-use).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does AppBlips use prompt caching to lower my Anthropic bill?">
    Yes. With Anthropic, AppBlips turns on [prompt caching](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching) by itself. There's nothing to set up.

    A build takes several steps, and each step sends the AI's instructions and the conversation so far again. Anthropic keeps those repeated parts for a few minutes and bills them at a small fraction of the normal price when the next step reuses them. Storing them costs a little extra the first time, so caching pays off from the second step on.

    If you run AppBlips from source or with Docker, you can watch it work in the `[anthropic]` lines the server prints. See [Find the error details](/reference/troubleshooting#find-the-error-details). Other providers handle caching their own way.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Projects and exporting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can I export what I build?">
    Yes. Click the download key next to **Publish** to download an app or game as a single HTML file, or a website as a `.zip` of its pages. Apps from the App studio can also download as a React project. See [Export your project](/features/exporting).

    Exported files open in any modern web browser, with no server needed. Some load styling or libraries, such as Tailwind CSS or a game engine, from the internet, so they need a connection.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I undo a change?">
    Click **Previous version** (the left arrow in the preview toolbar), or press `Ctrl+Z` (`⌘Z` on a Mac), to step back one change at a time. To jump to any earlier version, open the history drawer from the header. See [Undo and history](/features/live-preview).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Where are my projects saved?">
    - **Desktop app:** in a folder on your computer, one folder per app. To see where, open **Settings → Data** and look under **Projects folder**; click **Change…** to pick another one. By default it's inside AppBlips' own data folder, which Windows deletes if you uninstall or reset the Microsoft Store version, so export a backup first.
    - **Browser version:** in your web browser, separately for each address. Clearing your browser's data for that address deletes them, so back up now and then with **Settings → Data → Export**.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Privacy

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What does AppBlips connect to?">
    AppBlips sends no analytics or tracking. It connects to these services, and each can see your IP address when it does:

    - **Your AI provider** (the one in **Settings → AI**): your prompts, your project's code and any images you attach go there to build your app.
    - **The AppBlips server** (browser version only): the page sends its AI and extension requests to the server it was opened from. When you run AppBlips from source or Docker, that server is on your own computer. On web.appblips.com, it's AppBlips' server, which passes your requests on without storing your projects or keys. With **Free (no key needed)**, it sends them to the AI provider chosen by whoever runs that copy.
    - **GitHub:** to check for new versions, when AppBlips starts and every hour while it's open. The desktop app also downloads updates from there. Turn this off under **Settings → Workspace → Check for updates**. The Microsoft Store version gets updates from the Store and doesn't check GitHub.
    - **Google Fonts:** for the fonts AppBlips itself uses.
    - **Hugging Face:** only when you build AppBlips from source or with Docker, to download the voice-input speech model once. The desktop app already includes it.
    - **Code libraries:** the apps you build, and imported sites, load libraries such as React, Tailwind CSS or game engines from public sites like esm.sh, jsDelivr and cdn.tailwindcss.com. Exported apps load them from there too.
    - **Web3Forms:** if you turn on the Web3Forms extension, the sites you build send their form entries there whenever someone submits a form, including in the preview. AppBlips itself only contacts it when you use **Request an extension** or submit a project to the [Showcase](/features/showcase), and only sends what you typed in that form.
    - **Umami** and **Plausible:** only if you turn on that extension. AppBlips itself never contacts them. The pages you build load their tracking script, including in the preview, but preview visits aren't counted. Once your pages are online, they report each visit to your dashboard.
    - **tawk.to:** only if you turn on its extension. AppBlips itself never contacts it. The sites you build load its chat bubble, including in the preview, and send it what visitors type.
    - **Square:** only if you turn on its extension. AppBlips itself never contacts it. The buy and pay buttons in your sites open your Square checkout when someone clicks them, including in the preview.
    - **Pollinations:** only if you turn on its extension. AppBlips contacts it to sign you in, to show how much Pollen you have left, to make the images you ask for, to check a site key you paste, and to download your images when you publish or export. The sites you build contact it when they show those images and when visitors use AI features, including in the preview. See [Pollinations](/features/connectors#pollinations).
    - **Google:** only if you turn on the Google Sheets Backend extension. AppBlips contacts your script when you click **Test connection**. The sites you build send entries to your script and read results from it, including in the preview. Everything is kept in your own Google Sheet. See [Google Sheets Backend](/features/connectors#google-sheets-backend).
    - **Pixabay:** only if you turn on its extension. AppBlips contacts it when you save your key, when you search (sending your search words and key) and when you add photos (downloading them into your project). Search thumbnails load straight from Pixabay. Your pages don't contact Pixabay: the photos become part of your project. See [Pixabay](/features/connectors#pixabay).
    - **Tavily:** only if you turn on its extension. AppBlips contacts it when you save your key and when the AI searches the web, sending the AI's search words and your key, never your project's code. See [Tavily](/features/connectors#tavily).
    - **Your website, for SEO:** only if you type your website address into the SEO questions and agree to a scan. AppBlips then visits that one page once and keeps only its title, description and headings. See [SEO](/features/tools#seo).
    - **Surge:** only when you use **Publish** or sign in on the **Surge** card. AppBlips sends Surge your email and password when you sign in, and your project's pages and images when you publish. See [Publish to the web](/features/publishing).

    Voice input connects to nothing: the speech model runs on your computer, and your recording never leaves it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can other people see my projects?">
    No, not unless you share them. Your projects are saved on your computer (desktop app) or in your own browser (browser version). Nobody else using the same server, such as web.appblips.com, can see them. A project only goes online when you [publish](/features/publishing) it or submit it to the [Showcase](/features/showcase).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Installing

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Windows warns me about the installer or blocks it">
    This can happen with an old installer downloaded from GitHub, which wasn't code-signed. Install AppBlips from the [Microsoft Store](https://apps.microsoft.com/detail/9nk9ss4v2n1k) instead. It's the only way to install AppBlips on Windows, it's signed by Microsoft, and the Store keeps it up to date. See [Windows installer is blocked](/reference/troubleshooting#windows-installer-is-blocked).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
