Short answers to the questions people ask most.

## About AppBlips

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What can AppBlips build?">
    Apps, websites and 2D or 3D browser games, from a description in plain English: tools, calculators, quizzes, dashboards, portfolio sites, business sites, arcade games and more.

    Apps and games are single HTML files that run in any browser. Websites can have several pages and export as a `.zip`. Apps from the App studio can also export as a React project.

    AppBlips doesn't build native phone apps or apps that need their own server. To save what visitors send, such as RSVPs or votes, try the [Google Sheets Backend](/features/connectors#google-sheets-backend) extension.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do I need to know how to code?">
    No. If you can describe what you want, you can build it. If you do code, you can read, change and export everything AppBlips writes. Clear, specific prompts give better first results.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is AppBlips free?">
    Yes. AppBlips is free and open source under the [MIT License](https://github.com/techmitten/app-blips/blob/master/LICENSE), including for commercial work.

    The AI is the only part that can cost money. With your own API key, your AI provider bills you for what you use. On [web.appblips.com](/quickstart-browser), the **Free (no key needed)** option lets you build without a key, within limits.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is there a limit on how many apps I can build?">
    AppBlips sets none. With your own key, only your provider's limits and your budget apply. **Free (no key needed)** has usage limits set by whoever runs it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What's the difference between the desktop app and the browser version?">
    They're the same app, with projects and keys kept in different places:

    - **[Desktop app](/quickstart-desktop)** (Windows and Linux): saves projects as folders on your computer and keeps your key in your system's keychain.
    - **Browser version** ([web.appblips.com](/quickstart-browser), [from source](/quickstart-source) or [Docker](/self-hosting/docker)): saves projects and keys in your browser, for that address only.

    To move projects between them, use **Settings → Data → Export** in one and **Import…** in the other.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I use AppBlips on a Mac?">
    Yes, through the browser version. The easiest way is [web.appblips.com](/quickstart-browser). To run your own copy, use [Pinokio](https://pinokio.co/apps/github-com-techmitten-app-blips-pinokio), [source](/quickstart-source) or [Docker](/self-hosting/docker). There's no Mac desktop app.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## AI and API keys

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I need an API key?">
    Usually, yes. An API key is a secret code from an AI provider that lets AppBlips use your account there. Paste it into AppBlips once. See [Choose an AI provider](/ai-providers).

    You don't need one if your copy of AppBlips offers **Free (no key needed)**, as [web.appblips.com](/quickstart-browser) does.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Which AI providers can I use?">
    Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini, xAI, DeepSeek and OpenRouter. Open **Settings → AI**, choose one, paste your key, pick a model and click **Save**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What happens to my API key?">
    - **Desktop app:** encrypted with your system's keychain and kept only on your computer. On Linux without a keychain, the protection is weak, and AppBlips warns you.
    - **Browser version:** kept in your browser, which sends it straight to your provider. It never reaches the AppBlips server.

    Your key is never written into your apps, projects or exports. See [Security](/self-hosting/security#your-api-key).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How can I see how much I'm using?">
    AppBlips doesn't show a count. Your AI provider's usage or billing page does. See [How much does a build use?](/features/building-apps#how-much-does-a-build-use).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does AppBlips use prompt caching to lower my Anthropic bill?">
    Yes. With Anthropic, AppBlips turns on [prompt caching](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching) for you. A build takes several steps that each resend the same instructions, and Anthropic bills the repeated parts at a lower price. If you run AppBlips yourself, you can see it in the `[anthropic]` log lines. See [Find the error details](/reference/troubleshooting#find-the-error-details).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Projects and exporting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can I export what I build?">
    Yes. Click the download button next to **Publish**. Apps and games download as one HTML file, websites as a `.zip`, and App studio apps can also download as a React project. See [Export your project](/features/exporting).

    Exported files open in any modern browser. Some load styling or libraries, such as Tailwind CSS or a game engine, from the internet, so they need a connection.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I undo a change?">
    Click **Previous version** (the left arrow in the preview toolbar) or press `Ctrl+Z` (`⌘Z` on a Mac). To jump to any earlier version, open the history drawer in the header. See [Undo and history](/features/live-preview).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Where are my projects saved?">
    - **Desktop app:** in a folder on your computer, one folder per app. See where under **Settings → Data → Projects folder**, and click **Change…** to move it. The default folder lives in AppBlips' data folder, which the Microsoft Store version loses if you uninstall or reset it, so export a backup first.
    - **Browser version:** in your browser, separately for each address. Clearing browser data for that address deletes them, so export a backup now and then.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Privacy

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What does AppBlips connect to?">
    AppBlips has no analytics or tracking. It connects to the services below, and each can see your IP address when it does.

    **Always, or as part of normal use**

    - **Your AI provider** (the one in **Settings → AI**): receives your prompts, your project's code and any images you attach, to build your app.
    - **The AppBlips server** (browser version): the server the page was opened from. For a copy you run yourself, that's your own computer. On web.appblips.com it's AppBlips' server. With your own key, AI requests and Tavily and Pixabay searches go straight from your browser to those services, not through the server. The server handles **Free (no key needed)**, using the AI provider chosen by whoever runs that copy, and passes on Publish (Surge) and Pollinations requests, which those services don't accept from a browser. It doesn't store your projects or keys.
    - **Free AI sign-in (Clerk):** only if your copy asks you to sign in to use **Free (no key needed)**. Sign-in uses Clerk with your Google or GitHub account.
    - **GitHub:** to check for new versions at startup and every hour. The AppImage also downloads updates from there. Turn this off in **Settings → Workspace → Check for updates**. The Snap version checks the Snap Store instead, and the Microsoft Store version only checks GitHub for announcements.
    - **AppBlips website (appblips.com):** the app checks the date of the Privacy Policy and Terms of Service so it can ask you to agree again if they change. Links to the policies, Showcase and docs open there.
    - **Google Fonts:** for the fonts AppBlips uses.
    - **Code libraries:** the apps you build, and imported sites, load libraries such as React, Tailwind CSS or game engines from public sites like esm.sh, jsDelivr and cdn.tailwindcss.com. Exported apps load them from there too.
    - **Hugging Face:** the first time you use voice input, after you agree, AppBlips downloads the speech model (about 76 MB) from there. It's kept on your device, and your recordings never leave it.

    **Only when you use the feature**

    - **Built-in photo library (Unsplash):** when the AI looks for photos, your search words go to the AppBlips server, which asks Unsplash. Your pages then load those photos from Unsplash.
    - **Web search:** when web search is on, the AI sends your provider (or Tavily, if connected) its search words, never your project's code. On DeepSeek it can also read a page address.
    - **Surge:** when you sign in or **Publish**. Sign-in sends Surge your email and password, and publishing sends your pages and images. See [Publish to the web](/features/publishing).
    - **Public DNS (Cloudflare, Google):** when you check a custom domain in Publish. Only the domain name is sent.
    - **Your own website, for SEO:** only if you type your website address into the SEO questions and agree to a scan. AppBlips visits that page once and keeps only its title, description and headings.
    - **Web3Forms:** **Send feedback**, **Request an extension** and submitting a project to the [Showcase](/features/showcase) send what you typed in that form. If you turn on the Web3Forms extension, your sites also send form entries there when someone submits one, including in the preview.

    **Only if you turn on the extension**

    - **Umami, Plausible, tawk.to, Square:** AppBlips itself never contacts them. The sites you build load their scripts or open their checkout, including in the preview. Preview visits aren't counted in Umami or Plausible.
    - **Pollinations:** AppBlips contacts it to sign you in, show your Pollen balance, make images and download them when you publish or export. Your sites contact it to show those images and for AI features. See [Pollinations](/features/connectors#pollinations).
    - **Google Sheets Backend:** AppBlips contacts your script when you click **Test connection**. Your sites send entries to your script and read results from it. Everything stays in your own Google Sheet.
    - **Pixabay:** AppBlips contacts it when you save your key, search (sending your search words and key) and add photos (downloaded into your project). Your pages don't contact Pixabay.
    - **Tavily:** AppBlips contacts it when you save your key and when the AI searches, sending the search words and your key, never your project's code.

    Voice input itself sends nothing: the recording is turned into text on your device.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can other people see my projects?">
    No, unless you share them. Projects stay on your computer (desktop app) or in your browser (browser version). Nobody else using the same server, such as web.appblips.com, can see them. A project only goes online when you [publish](/features/publishing) it or submit it to the [Showcase](/features/showcase).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Installing

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Windows warns me about the installer or blocks it">
    This can happen with an old installer downloaded from GitHub, which wasn't code-signed. Install AppBlips from the [Microsoft Store](https://apps.microsoft.com/detail/9nk9ss4v2n1k) instead. It's signed by Microsoft and kept up to date by the Store. See [Windows installer is blocked](/reference/troubleshooting#windows-installer-is-blocked).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
