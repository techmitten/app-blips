You don't need to code. Describe your idea in everyday words, and the AI builds it. A minute or so later it's running in the preview, and you can keep chatting to change it.

## Pick a studio

Every new project starts by choosing what to make:

- **Mobile & Web App** for tools, dashboards and utilities, like a budget tracker, quiz or timer. An app is one page.
- **Responsive Website** for sites with a menu and several pages, like a business, portfolio or event site. See [Multi-page websites](/features/multi-page-websites).
- **Browser Game** for 2D and 3D games you play with a keyboard, mouse or touch.

The same screen also links to **My projects** (your saved work), **Import a site** (see [Import a site](/features/importing-sites)) and **Explore** (see [Showcase](/features/showcase)). To start something new later, click **New** in the header.

## Write your first prompt

A prompt is the message that tells the AI what to build.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Describe your idea">
    Type it in the box. For example: "A tip calculator. I enter the bill, pick a tip percentage and the number of people, and it shows the tip and the total per person."
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send it">
    Press Enter, or click the button under the box. Shift+Enter starts a new line. On a touchscreen keyboard, Enter always starts a new line, so tap the button.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch it build">
    The preview shows progress, then your app appears. See [Live preview](/features/live-preview#while-your-app-builds). To stop part-way, click **Cancel**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Tips for better results

- **List the features you want.** "A to-do list where I can add tasks, tick them off and filter by done." The AI builds what you ask for and doesn't guess at extras.
- **Describe the look.** Colors, layout or mood: "dark navy background, white text" or "calm and minimal, like a notes app".
- **Say what your data looks like.** "Each task has a title, due date and priority."
- **Say what clicks should do.** "Clicking a card opens its full description."
- **Start small, then add.** A short prompt followed by a few changes often beats one huge prompt. Every change is saved as a version, so you can step back.

### Starter ideas

Not sure what to build? A new project shows ideas under the prompt box, headed **Or start with one of these**. Click one to put its prompt in the box, change it if you like, then send it. Use **Previous ideas** and **More ideas** to browse.

## Add a picture to your prompt

A sketch, a screenshot of a site you like or a mockup can say more than words.

- **From your device:** click the paperclip (**Attach an image from your device**).
- **From your clipboard:** copy an image, then press Ctrl+V (⌘V on a Mac) in the prompt box.
- **Of your app:** once something is built, click the camera (**Attach a screenshot of the preview**). It's a quick way to show a problem, like "the Sign up button is cut off".

A thumbnail appears above the box. Click it to enlarge it, or click **Remove attached image**. Then write your message as usual.

<Note>
  In the browser version, pictures need your own API key. With **Free (no key needed)**, the paperclip and camera aren't shown and pasted images aren't attached. See [Free AI](/ai-providers#free-ai).
</Note>

## Speak your prompt

On a new project's start screen you can say your idea instead of typing it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start recording">
    Click the microphone (**Speak your prompt**). If asked, allow microphone access. See [Microphone permission](#microphone-permission).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Talk">
    While **Listening…** shows, describe what you want.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stop">
    Click the microphone again (**Stop voice input**). After **Transcribing…**, your words appear in the prompt box.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check and send">
    Fix any misheard words, then send. Recording stops by itself after 2 minutes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Speech is turned into text by Whisper, an open-source model that runs on your own computer. Your recording is never uploaded or saved, and it works offline. The first recording after you open AppBlips takes a few extra seconds while the model loads. It can mishear unusual names, so read the text before sending.

### Microphone permission

- **Installer from GitHub, or Linux:** AppBlips doesn't ask. On Windows, make sure the microphone switches in Windows Settings are on.
- **Microsoft Store version:** Windows asks the first time. Click **Yes**.
- **Browser version:** your browser asks the first time. Click **Allow**.

If you see `Microphone access is blocked`, see [Troubleshooting](/reference/troubleshooting).

## Keep changing it

After the first build, describe a change and AppBlips updates your app instead of starting over:

```
Add a dark mode switch in the top-right corner.
```

Each change is saved as a version. If one misses, click **Previous version** in the preview toolbar or press Ctrl+Z. See [Versions and history](/features/live-preview#versions-and-history). To change things by clicking on the page, see [Edit your app](/features/editing-apps).

Useful chat keys:

- **Copy message** copies a message's text.
- **Rewind project to this message** (on your earlier messages) takes the project back to that point.
- **New chat (keeps version history)** (the **+** key) starts a fresh conversation. It helps when a long chat has drifted off track.

To get ideas for next steps, turn on **Suggested prompts** in **Settings → AI**. After each build, short suggestions appear above the prompt box. It's off by default because it uses a few extra tokens.

## Ask questions without changing anything

The key next to the paperclip switches the prompt box between **Build** and **Ask**. In **Ask**, the AI only answers, so you can ask how something works without touching your app. In **Build**, a plain question like "how does the score get saved?" is answered without changes, but a request phrased as a question, like "can you add a dark mode?", is built.

## Finish the setup

Some apps need something only you can provide, such as an API key (a password-like code that lets an app use an online service). Then the AI's message ends with **To finish setting it up:** and numbered steps. Usually the last step is to paste the value into the chat and ask the AI to add it.

<Warning>
  Anyone who opens your app can see what's inside it, including a key. Use a free key, or one you can restrict, never one tied to a paid account you can't limit.
</Warning>

## Let the AI look things up

The AI's knowledge can be out of date. To let it search the web for current facts, turn on the [Tavily extension](/features/connectors#tavily) with your own free Tavily key. The chat shows **Searching the web…** while it looks. Check important facts before you publish.

## Use your own images

To build with your own photos or logo, turn on **My images** and add pictures (**Settings → Tools**). Then mention an image by name, like "put team-photo in the hero section". The AI shows which image it matched and waits for you to click **Yes, use it**. See [My images](/features/tools#my-images-beta).

## Settings that shape a build

These are in **Settings → AI**.

### Reasoning

Reasoning lets the AI think before it writes code. It helps with tricky logic, but it's slower and uses more tokens. Pick a level under **Reasoning**, from **Off** up to **Max**. The list shows only what your provider and model support. The default is **Medium**. Ask mode and clarifying questions never use reasoning.

### Clarifying questions

Turn on **Clarifying questions** and the AI may ask a few short questions before building when your prompt leaves room for doubt. It's off by default.

### Game engines

In the Game studio, AppBlips picks a game engine (a ready-made toolkit) for each new game:

- **Phaser** for 2D games with lots of moving sprites, like platformers and shooters.
- **Three.js** for anything 3D.
- **No engine** (plain canvas) for simple arcade, puzzle, card and board games, so they load faster.

The reply tells you which one it chose. To choose yourself, name it in your prompt, such as "make it in Three.js". Your choice always wins. The engine is set on the first build. To let the AI decide as it writes, turn off **Smart game engine**.

### Website add-ons

In the Website studio, AppBlips adds ready-made libraries only when a site clearly needs them:

- **Swiper** for carousels and sliders.
- **GLightbox** for full-screen photos.
- **GSAP** or **AOS** for scroll animations (never both).
- **Lenis** for smooth scrolling.
- **Alpine.js** for tabs, pop-ups and price switches.
- **Chart.js** for charts.
- **Leaflet** for maps.

A site gets at most three, and simple sites usually get none. Name a library in your prompt to choose yourself, or say "no libraries". To build with plain JavaScript only, turn off **Smart website add-ons**.

## Automatic error fixing

After each build, AppBlips checks the code and watches for errors while your app runs. If it finds one, it asks the AI to fix it. The preview shows **Auto Error Checking**, and you can click **Cancel** to stop. The fix is saved as a version, so you can undo it.

AppBlips only tries a few times. If it can't fix the problem, describe what's wrong in the chat or go back to an earlier version. It doesn't auto-fix errors in code you edited yourself. See [Edit the code yourself](/features/live-preview#edit-the-code-yourself).

## Very large apps

Each AI reply has a length limit. It's set under **Output tokens: building** in **Settings → AI** (64,000 tokens by default, enough for a large app). If you use the Free option on a server that sets the limit itself, the field isn't shown. If a reply hits the limit, AppBlips asks the AI to carry on, up to three times, and tells you if the app is still unfinished. See [Troubleshooting](/reference/troubleshooting).

## How much does a build use?

AI providers bill by tokens. A token is a small piece of text, about three-quarters of a word. One build can be several requests, and each one sends your message, your app's code and the chat so far.

AppBlips shows an estimate of the tokens the current chat has used, for example **Est. 12.4k tokens**. On a computer it's in the Build pane's header. On a phone or tablet it's just above the prompt box. It counts up while the AI works, with a green dot while a build is running, and starts again from zero when you start a new chat.

Click the counter to open a breakdown. A bar shows how the total splits into these parts:

- **Input:** what was sent to the AI that wasn't cached: your message, your app's code and AppBlips' instructions.
- **Cache reads:** input the AI reused from its cache, usually at a lower price. See [Prompt caching](/costs/prompt-caching).
- **Cache writes:** input saved to the AI's cache so later requests can reuse it.
- **Output:** what the AI wrote, including any thinking.
- **Unreported:** an estimate for requests the provider didn't report on. While a build runs, this is shown as **Still counting** until the exact counts arrive.

If you click **Cancel** part-way through, the provider doesn't report that request's tokens, but you're still billed for the part it did. AppBlips keeps as much of the real count as it can:

- **Claude:** the input is counted exactly. Only the output is estimated, and it may be low, because Claude shows a summary of its thinking rather than all of it.
- **OpenRouter:** AppBlips asks OpenRouter for the request's real counts a few seconds later, and the counter updates to match.
- **Other providers:** the request is estimated from the text that came back. The **Unreported** row says how many stopped requests it includes. Their estimate may be low, because thinking the AI didn't show in full isn't counted.

Parts with nothing in them aren't shown. Below the bar you'll also see:

- **Your last message:** what your most recent request used. While a build runs, it's **This message so far**.
- **AI requests:** how many requests the chat has made, and how many of them the provider didn't report on.

Press **Esc** or click anywhere else to close the breakdown. With Free AI, nothing is billed to you, but the counter still shows what was used.

<Warning>
The counter is an estimate only, not a bill. It adds up the counts your AI provider reports, and providers price cached input, output and web searches differently. Some providers don't report every request, and a request you stop part-way is partly estimated. Always check your provider's usage or billing page for what you actually pay.
</Warning>

To compare providers and models, see [AI providers](/ai-providers).
