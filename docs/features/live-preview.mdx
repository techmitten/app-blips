The preview sits next to the chat. As soon as a build finishes, your app appears there and works like the real thing: click buttons, fill in forms, play the game. There's nothing to install or upload.

## While your app builds

During a build, the preview shows a building screen drawn to look like the device you're previewing on, with a clock and your project's name across the top. It lists each stage and ticks them off as the AI works:

1. **Understanding your idea**
2. **Reasoning** (only when the AI is thinking, see [Reasoning](/features/building-apps#reasoning))
3. **Writing the code**, with the name of the page being written on a multi-page website, and which page of how many it is
4. **Checking for errors**
5. **Finishing up**

At the bottom, a timer counts the seconds and a counter shows how many lines of code have been written so far.

### Watch the code being written

Click **Show code** at the bottom of the building screen to watch the code appear line by line. When the AI is thinking, a **thinking** strip under the code shows what it's considering. Click **Hide code** to go back to the progress view.

If you'd rather not see the code at all, turn off **Live code preview** in **Settings → Workspace**. The **Show code** button is then hidden.

If AppBlips finds an error after a build, the screen changes to **Auto Error Checking** while it fixes it, with a **Cancel** button to stop. See [Automatic error fixing](/features/building-apps#automatic-error-fixing).

## Choose a device view

The keys in the middle of the preview toolbar change the size of screen your app is shown on. Hover over a key to see its name and size.

- **Smartphone:** your app inside a phone frame.
- **Tablet:** your app inside a tablet frame.
- **Desktop:** your app at desktop width. It opens in **Full size**.

Next to them:

- **Rotate** (a square with a curved arrow) turns the phone or tablet between portrait and landscape.
- **Full size** (four arrows pointing outward) works in the Desktop view and is on by default. Your app fills the whole preview area at its real size, the way it looks in a desktop browser. Click it to see your app in a fixed-size desktop window instead, scaled to fit. Click it again to go back.
- **Reload** (a circular arrow) restarts your app from the beginning, in any view.

A key that doesn't apply to the current view is greyed out. Apps and games open in the Smartphone view and websites in Full size, but you can switch at any time. AppBlips remembers your choice.

### Zoom

The zoom keys (**Zoom out**, **Zoom in**) are on the right of the toolbar. Zoom out to see a whole layout at once, or zoom in to check fine details. The box between them shows **Auto** while the preview fits the pane, or the zoom level, such as **80%**. Click it to go back to fitting the pane.

Zoom is greyed out in Full size, which always shows your app at its real size.

### In a narrow window or on a phone

When the AppBlips window is narrow, some preview keys move into the **Preview tools** menu (a sliders icon), and the chat and the preview take turns on screen. Switch between them with **Chat** and **Preview** at the top.

On a phone or tablet, AppBlips always shows your project the way it looks on that device, filling the screen. The device keys are hidden there. Your chosen view is kept for when you open the project on a computer.

## Browser bar and pages

In the Desktop and Full size views, a browser bar sits above your app, like the top of a web browser:

- **Back**, **Forward** and **Reload** work inside your app, the same as in a browser.
- The box in the middle shows your project's name. You can't type in it.

The phone and tablet views leave the browser bar out, so they look like the real device. Use the **Reload** key in the toolbar there.

For an [imported site](/features/importing-sites), the box shows the current address, such as `appblips.local/about`. You can type another address there and press Enter to go to it.

When a website has more than one page, a page picker appears in the preview toolbar. Choose a page from it to open that page.

## Open it in a new tab

To try your app in a full browser window, click **Open in a new tab** (a square with an arrow, on the right of the toolbar). It opens your app's current version in a window of its own. This key isn't there for imported sites.

## Read the code

To see the code behind your app, click **Code** at the left of the preview toolbar. It shows each page's full source, with colors to make it easier to read. Click **Preview** to go back to your app.

Two settings in **Settings → Workspace** change the Code tab:

- **Code view** shows or hides the **Code** tab. It's on by default.
- **Auto Follow Code** scrolls the Code tab along with the newest line while a build is running. It's on by default.

For an imported site, this tab is called **Files** and shows every file in the project. See [Import a site](/features/importing-sites).

### Edit the code yourself

If you know some HTML, CSS or JavaScript, you can change the code by hand:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the page">
    Click **Code** and pick the page you want to change.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start editing">
    Click **Edit** at the top right of the code.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make your change">
    Type your changes. Tab indents the line.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save**, or press Ctrl+S (⌘S on a Mac). To throw your changes away instead, click **Cancel**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You can't edit while the AI is working, so wait for it to finish first.

Each save becomes a new version, so **Previous version** undoes it like any other change. If you switch to the preview before saving, your unsaved changes are kept until you come back. Switching to another page first asks whether to discard them.

If your change breaks something, the chat says where, for example:

> Your code edit has an error at line 18: Uncaught ReferenceError: score is not defined. Fix it in the Code tab, or ask the AI to fix it.

AppBlips doesn't ask the AI to fix errors in your own edits by itself. That way your work is never overwritten, and no tokens are spent unless you ask. Fix it in the Code tab, or ask the AI in the chat.

### Autocomplete

While you edit, a short list of suggested words appears under the cursor:

- Press the up and down arrow keys to choose a suggestion.
- Press Enter or Tab to insert it, or click it.
- Keep typing to ignore the list, or press Escape to close it.

Suggestions come from words already in your code, plus common JavaScript keywords, HTML tags and attributes, and CSS properties. They're worked out on your computer, so nothing is sent to the AI and no tokens are used. To turn them off, switch off **Code autocomplete** in **Settings → Workspace**.

## Versions and history

Every build, every change the AI makes and every edit you make yourself is saved as a new version of your project. Nothing is lost when you try something new.

### Step back and forward

The preview toolbar shows which version you're on, such as **v3/5** (version 3 of 5), between two curved arrows:

- **Previous version** (the arrow on the left) steps back one version.
- **Next version** (the arrow on the right) steps forward again.

The keyboard works too: Ctrl+Z steps back, and Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z steps forward (⌘Z and ⌘⇧Z on a Mac).

If you step back and then ask for a new change, the new version is built on the one you're looking at.

### The history drawer

To jump straight to any version, click the panel key in the header, next to **Projects** (its tooltip says **Show history drawer**). In a narrow window, open the menu (☰) and click **History**. The drawer lists every version, grouped by chat, with the message that made it and the time. Click a version to restore it.

Versions are saved with the project, so they're still there the next time you open it.

## Your app runs in a safe space

The preview is sandboxed: your app runs walled off from AppBlips. It can't read your other projects, your settings or your API keys, and it can't change AppBlips.

Your app can still save things the way a website does, for example a high score or a to-do list. That data belongs to your project only and isn't shared with your other apps.
